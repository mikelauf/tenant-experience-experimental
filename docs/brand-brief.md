# Transamerica Pyramid — Brand Brief (building experience, powered by Playbook)

This is a design study. Venues, schedules, people and terms are demonstration content.

## Concept
The Transamerica Pyramid at 600 Montgomery Street, San Francisco: the 48-story tapered tower completed in 1972, with a half-acre grove of coast redwoods (Redwood Park) at its base. The building's experience program is run by Playbook, a hospitality and workplace-experience company. The digital front door treats the tower as a *vertical city*: a stack of places to work, meet, gather, train and recover, from the redwood grove at street level to the Bay Lounge on level 27.

Audiences: people who work in the building (members) and outside organizers looking for an event venue.

## Places
- **Bay Lounge (L27):** a hospitality lounge with curved upholstered seating, walnut and warm plaster, floor-to-ceiling windows over the Bay Bridge and the Bay. Receptions up to 130.
- **Redwood Park:** the half-acre grove of mature coast redwoods at the base, with stone paths, benches and a small fountain. Evening events under string lights.
- **Montgomery Hall:** a daylight-filled conference hall with pale oak and acoustic felt.
- **Member meeting rooms:** Clay, Jackson, Washington and Merchant, named after surrounding streets.
- **Pyramid Fitness:** strength floor, a Ride studio, a pilates and mobility studio, and a recovery lounge (cold plunge, cedar sauna, compression).

## Voice
Warm, precise, hospitable, like a great concierge. Sentence case. Short, specific, helpful. No hype, no "elevate", no "unlock".

## Visual identity
- Ground: quartz white #F2F0EB (the precast facade), fog #E4E5E1. Ink #111315. Evening sections: night #0D1012.
- One accent: redwood #9A3F25, used for actions and live state.
- Typeface: Instrument Sans (one family), sentence case. No serif, no monospace, no all-caps.

## Photography direction
San Francisco light: clean morning daylight, real golden hour, blue hour and low marine fog. Documentary hospitality photography that is observational and candid, never posed stock, never flash. True color, gently warm and calm, soft contrast, fine film-like grain, generous negative space. Materials: quartz-white concrete, walnut, pale oak, brass accents, bouclé and wool upholstery, redwood bark, cedar. Diverse working professionals of all ages, dressed in smart-casual neutrals (camel, charcoal, navy, cream, olive) with an occasional muted rust tone. Interiors glow with warm practical light against cool city or bay views. No readable text, no signage, no logos.
